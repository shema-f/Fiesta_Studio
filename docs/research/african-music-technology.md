# African Music Technology & Cultural Heritage Research

## 1. Context & Rationale

Digital Audio Workstations historically codified Western music theory into software defaults:
- Chromatic 12-tone equal temperament (12-TET) tuning.
- 4/4 time signatures with binary subdivisions (quarter, eighth, 16th notes).
- Standard European orchestral and American pop drum instrumentation.

Across Africa—and notably in Rwanda and East Africa—musical traditions embody sophisticated polyrhythmic interleaving, additive time structures, and microtonal acoustic tunings that do not conform neatly to rigid Western quantization grids.

**FIesta Studio** seeks to pioneer open-source tools that honor and elevate these traditions as foundational elements of modern digital music technology.

## 2. Cultural Instruments in FIesta Studio

### The Rwandan Inanga (Trough Zither)
The Inanga is one of the most venerable chordophones of Rwanda and the Great Lakes region:
- **Construction:** A flat, boat-shaped trough hollowed from single hardwood timber, strung with a continuous monofilament or gut cord wrapped across 6 to 9 notches.
- **Tuning:** Pentatonic system historically tuned by ear relative to the singer's vocal register.
- **Timbre:** Warm acoustic pluck with subtle percussive finger taps on the soundboard.
- **Implementation in FIesta:** Modeled using physical synthesis algorithms combining resonant bandpass filters and exponential decay envelopes.

### The Amayugi (Ceremonial Ankle Bells)
Worn by traditional Rwandan Intore dancers, the Amayugi create a rhythmic metallic shimmer that emphasizes syncopated steps.
- **Implementation:** Granular noise burst synthesis with multi-pole metallic bandpass resonances.

## 3. Modern African Electronic Genres: Amapiano & Afrobeats

FIesta Studio provides native support for modern African electronic genres:
- **Amapiano Log Drum:** A synthesized, pitch-bent FM sub-bass instrument characterized by sudden percussive attack, rapid semitone slides, and warm saturation.
- **Afrobeat Polyrhythmic Swing:** Micro-timing shifts that interleave 3-against-2 and 6-against-4 syncopation.
